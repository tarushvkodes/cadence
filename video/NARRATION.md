## Read-through

**Cadence**

This is Cadence. We built it to help you plan your week around how you actually feel. It's for women 17 to 25, and it runs in the browser.

**The idea**

When we started, we assumed the cycle changes how well people think. Then we read the research, and it doesn't back that up. A review of 102 studies found no robust change in cognitive performance across the cycle. What can change is how you feel, and that differs from person to person. So Cadence plans around what you log, and treats your phase as a hint.

**Step 1: Set up**

Step 1 is setup. You tell it when your last period started, how long your cycle usually is, and how regular it is. Then it makes four promises. Your data stays on your computer, the AI only suggests, and it's not medical advice. And the dates are estimates, so don't use them for birth control. Then we tap Start with a sample week, which is a busy student week with a midterm on Thursday.

**Step 2: Read your day**

Step 2 is reading your day, on the Today screen. The dial says day 25 of about 28. Your next period is probably Nov 18 to 22. It gives you a range instead of a date, because only 13% of cycles are exactly 28 days. It also says how sure it is, and you can tap that to see why. Below is the 10-second check-in, for energy, mood, and symptoms. That's what our app plans around.

**Step 3: Ask the Guide**

Step 3 is asking the Guide. We tap "Plan my week around my cycle." The Guide is an agent that runs on Claude, which is a large language model. A plain chatbot only sees what you type into it. An agent also has tools. A tool is basically a small piece of code we wrote that the model can use. For instance, one of them reads your calendar. So the agent decides what it needs, calls a tool, reads the result, and repeats until it can propose changes. Here it uses four tools. It checks your cycle, it reads your calendar, your habits, and your check-ins. All of that goes to Claude only when you ask it something. Then it drafts changes, and a tool catches two that don't fit into your calendar, so the model fixes them. And it ends with five suggestions, each one explained with your own check-ins. For instance, it lightens Wednesday's hard workout, because that's probably around day 27, when you logged low energy and headaches.

**Step 4: You decide**

Step 4 is where you decide. Each suggestion shows what changes and why. We approve two, decline one, and then undo one of the approvals. On the Plan, anything still waiting shows up as a dashed block. Classes, exams, and shifts are locked, so the Guide can't move them. And the AI can't change your calendar on its own, so nothing changes until you approve it.

**Step 5: A rough morning**

Step 5 is a rough morning. It's Thursday, day 1 of your period, and your midterm is tonight. You log it in 10 seconds: low energy, flat mood, cramps. Then you ask the Guide what to change. It only touches today, with three small changes, and the midterm stays put.

**Step 6: Know the limits**

Step 6 is knowing its limits. You tell it your cramps are bad enough to miss class. It doesn't diagnose, because it's not a doctor. It tells you to get this checked by a doctor, and it explains when to go to urgent care. Then it offers to turn your check-ins into notes for the appointment, and it does.

**Step 7: See inside**

Step 7 is seeing inside. Our Learn page labels every claim as well supported, mixed, or weak, with its source. And Settings shows the instructions the AI follows, word for word.

**Remember**

So there's three things to remember. Cadence plans around how you feel, instead of your phase. The AI suggests, and you decide. And it knows when to send you to a real clinician. Our code is on GitHub, at the link on the screen. Next, we'll get into the ethics behind it.
